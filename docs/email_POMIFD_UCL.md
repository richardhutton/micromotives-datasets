Dear Dr Blumenau,

Your Harvard Dataverse deposit for "The Variable Persuasiveness of Political Rhetoric"
(doi:10.7910/DVN/POMIFD) says "You may wish to communicate with the Contact(s) specified
before use", so I am doing that.

I am building a UK dataset for testing how accurately language models predict human
survey responses, following SocSci210 (arXiv:2509.05830), which did this for 210 US
experiments. Each row pairs a respondent's demographics with the exact stimulus they saw
and the answer they gave.

Yours is the study I would most like to include. Nearly everything else I have is
conjoint, where the stimulus is a table of attribute levels and the quantity of interest
is an average effect. Your treatments are real political arguments and your question is
which of two a particular person found more persuasive — an individual-level prediction
problem with genuine political writing as the input. Given your own finding that
persuasiveness varies so much across implementations, it is also a much harder test than a
study built around a single argument.

I am not redistributing anything. Nothing derived from your data would be published or
shared with anyone; this is for my own research use, and the article would be cited.
