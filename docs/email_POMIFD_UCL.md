## Notes for rich, not for sending

**Why send it at all.** The deposit's terms explicitly invite contact before use. Asking
costs nothing and removes any ambiguity; not asking leaves an invitation declined on the
record. It is also the cheapest possible liability insurance — you are notifying, not
requesting, so there is nothing for them to refuse that would cost you anything.

**What changed from the first draft.** That version asked permission to republish their
336 argument texts and respondent-level answers inside an openly licensed dataset. That
was the right ask for a published dataset and the wrong one for yours. This version asks
for nothing and simply states what you are doing.

**If they do not reply**, that is not a refusal and the terms do not require consent —
they say "you may wish to communicate". You have.

**What we gain:** ~3,317 respondents and ~13,268 pairwise comparisons, the richest
persona block of any UK deposit, across 12 UK policy topics — Heathrow's third runway,
Boxing Day trading, Right to Buy, surveillance powers, fracking, rail nationalisation,
boardroom quotas, cannabis, tuition fees, Trident, the 0.7% aid commitment, the sugar
tax. That spread materially reduces the corpus's Brexit concentration.
